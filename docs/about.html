About the Project

Urban Air Quality Index & Pollutant Drift Analysis is a systematic academic investigation into the temporal drift of urban air pollutants and the predictive modeling of next-day air quality boundary crossings using statistical machine learning.

The project examines real-world observational sensor records across metropolitan and national monitoring panels in India, applying verifiable sub-index calculation rules, formal two-sample drift hypothesis testing with False Discovery Rate (FDR) control, and both supervised and unsupervised statistical machine learning methods.

Research Objectives

  • Verified-Subset Air Quality Index: Establish a defensible verified-subset Indian Air Quality Index from validated multi-sensor inputs satisfying strict completeness criteria.
  • Quantification of Pollutant Drift: Formally quantify standardized observational drift across seasonal transition windows using robust effect-size metrics.
  • Statistical Significance & Error Control: Assess drift significance through two-sample hypothesis testing, Benjamini-Hochberg False Discovery Rate (FDR) control, and non-parametric permutation/bootstrap inference.
  • Supervised Next-Day Classification: Develop and evaluate supervised learning approaches (Multiple Linear Regression, Logistic Regression, and Radial Basis Function Support Vector Machines) to predict next-day adverse-AQI boundary crossings.
  • Unsupervised Structure & Operational Regimes: Examine lower-dimensional multi-pollutant covariance structure via Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and discover operational regime clusters via K-Means.
  • Benchmark Comparison: Benchmark linear, logistic, and nonlinear machine learning architectures against persistence-aware domain baselines.

Study Design & Data Scope

The project monitors 21 unique physical CAAQMS stations partitioned into two panels:

  • Hyderabad Metropolitan Panel: 7 physical monitoring stations across the Hyderabad urban area.
  • India National Representative Panel: 15 physical monitoring stations representing key industrial, commercial, and background zones across India.
  • Overlapping Station: 1 station (PROJ_007 — Zoo Park, Hyderabad) belongs to both panel memberships simultaneously, explaining why 7 + 15 − 1 = 21 unique physical monitoring stations.

Temporal Study Window: 570 continuous calendar days from 1 March 2025 through 21 September 2026.
• Modeling History: 1 March 2025 through 31 August 2026 (used for train, validation, and test splits).
• Recent Holdout Evaluation: 1 September 2026 through 21 September 2026 (21-day frozen holdout).

Verified-Subset Air Quality Index Policy

The project computes a verified-subset Indian Air Quality Index using three core criteria pollutants: PM2.5, PM10, and 8-hour maximum O3, provided daily completeness rules are satisfied.

Unsupported source-scale gases (CO, NO2, SO2) are excluded from the composite index calculation because OpenAQ post-2022 feeds do not independently validate mass concentration unit conversions to regulatory standards.

Regulatory Boundary: The verified-subset Air Quality Index is an analytical research construct strictly aligned with Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) sub-index breakpoints; it must NOT be described as the official operational Central Pollution Control Board Air Quality Index.

Analytical Workflow

The research executes across 11 sequential, reproducible stages:

  1. Data Engineering & Quality Control (Multi-source ingestion, sensor validation, deduplication)
  2. Verified-Subset Air Quality Index Calculation (CPCB piecewise linear breakpoint sub-indices)
  3. Exploratory Data Analysis (Distributional assessment, seasonality, correlation structure)
  4. Pollutant Drift Analysis (Standardized drift score calculation across seasonal windows)
  5. Statistical Inference (Two-sample tests, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control, permutation testing)
  6. Multiple Linear Regression (Continuous next-day AQI prediction with persistence benchmarks)
  7. Logistic Regression (Next-day adverse-AQI classification, PR-AUC and Brier calibration)
  8. Principal Component Analysis (Orthogonal variance decomposition and loading analysis)
  9. K-Means Clustering (Multi-pollutant environmental regime discovery and profiling)
  10. Radial Basis Function Support Vector Machine (Nonlinear margin-based adverse-event classification)
  11. Static Scientific Presentation Website (Decoupled, zero-CDN, accessible interactive dissemination)

For mathematical equations, loss functions, and reproduction commands, see Documentation: Theoretical Concepts and Execution Guide.

Scientific Limitations

  • Verified-Subset Air Quality Index Limitation: The computed index reflects a conservative verified-subset derived from PM2.5, PM10, and O3. Gaseous pollutants (CO, NO2, SO2) were excluded because source feeds lacked independent mass concentration verification.
  • Observational Drift vs. Causal Attribution: Standardized drift metrics (Dz) quantify empirical mean shifts between temporal observation windows. They are descriptive indicators and do not establish causal atmospheric emission attribution.
  • Seasonal Prevalence Shift: Fixed-threshold classifiers experience sensitivity shifts when background adverse-event rates transition drastically across seasons; models utilize validation-selected operating thresholds (p* ≈ 0.311268 for Hyderabad, p* ≈ 0.713448 for India) rather than a naïve 0.50 cutoff.
  • Static Presentation Model: The public website visualizes precomputed, frozen scientific outputs and does not execute live model retraining in the browser.

Data Sources & Provenance

  • Air Quality: OpenAQ REST API v3 representing Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Stations (CAAQMS) associated with Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and State Pollution Control Board (SPCB) networks.
  • Meteorology: Open-Meteo Historical Weather API (hourly ambient temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, and wind direction).
  • Regulatory Reference: Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) National Air Quality Index methodology guidelines.
